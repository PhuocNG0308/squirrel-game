Quantum Squirrels Field Manual

A mining economy where the machines wear out, the coolant runs dry, and the squirrels in the canopy are paid to take what you did not defend. This manual specifies the sustainable-operations layer: emission, sinks, progression, and the retention systems that keep a testnet populated long enough to learn something from it.

A Ponzi pays you for depositing. A game pays you for playing. Every design decision below is an attempt to move reward from the first column into the second.
01

Design thesis

The previous economy failed a simple test. Its token sinks — repair, coolant, acorns — were all denominated in qBTC, so burning them destroyed a claim without adding any backing. Supply fell; nothing was worth more.

Burning is supply management, not value creation. What actually reduces dependence on new entrants is changing who is eligible to earn. Emission that scales with deposit size pays the largest wallet by construction — that is the Ponzi shape, whatever the sinks look like. Emission gated behind attention, skill and ranking pays the most engaged player instead, and an engaged player is one who is still here next month.

So the split below is the core economic claim of this document: half of all emission still flows to mining, but none of it flows to idle capital, and a further 40% is reachable only by playing.

Capital-weighted
50%

Mining yield — and even this pays nothing without fuel and maintenance.

Activity & rank gated
40%

Season ladders and quests. Unreachable by depositing alone.

Never emitted to players
10%

AMM liquidity and operations reserve.

03

Emission curve

Mining and season pools decay 4% per 30-day season, a geometric series that converges on its allocation without ever exhausting it. Quests pay flat across 60 seasons and then stop.

A halving schedule was rejected: its first epoch necessarily emits half the cap, front-loading the economy into exactly the early dump that kills these games. A gentler per-season decay spreads the same total over a usable life.

04

Three reward tracks

Each track answers a different question, and a player who ignores two of them leaves most of the economy on the table.

TrackAnswersScales withWhale advantage
MiningDid you maintain your machines?Live hashrateLinear, but so is the upkeep bill
SeasonsDid you outperform other players?Ladder rankNone — top rank pays a fixed share
QuestsDid you engage with the systems?CompletionNone — one-time per account

Mining is deliberately the least interesting track. It is the base load, it demands daily attention to earn anything at all, and it is the only place where owning more hardware helps.

05

Rig operations

A Quantum Rig is a machine, not a savings account. It degrades, it needs coolant, and it will destroy itself if run into the ground.

Durability

Every rig starts at 100 points and loses 5 per day of mining. Hashrate falls in bands, so decline is felt in steps rather than as a slow fade — a rig crossing into Overheating is a visible event worth reacting to.

DurabilityStateHashrateConsequence
75–100Optimal100%Nominal operation
50–74Overheating80%Coolant demand unchanged, output down
20–49Damaged50%Repair cost now material
1–19Critical20%10% chance per day of permanent loss; repairs cost 1.5×
0Shutdown0%Mines nothing; revivable at full cost
Fairness requirement

Destroying an NFT on a random roll must use commit–reveal. Without it a player wraps the call in a contract, reads the outcome, and reverts whenever the rig would have exploded — turning a 10% risk into zero.

Repair

Repair is priced against what the rig would earn in a day at full durability — never against its actual recent output. Pricing off actual output would make repair cost collapse toward zero exactly when a rig is most broken, so a player could let a machine die and revive it for nothing.

cost = points × 0.05 × fullRateDailyYield × penalty + 0.02 × fullRateDailyYield
penalty = 1.5 when durability ≤ 19, otherwise 1

The trailing flat term is a service fee per repair action, and it exists to create the maintenance decision. Without it, cost is purely linear in points, so topping up five points daily and repairing thirty points every sixth day cost precisely the same amount and there is nothing to decide.

StrategyRepairs atNet per rig, 360 dSurvival
Balanced74 points15,893100%
DiligentDaily15,309100%

Balanced narrowly beats Diligent: repairing less often saves service fees, just enough to offset the hashrate lost while Overheating. That margin is the knob — widening the service fee pushes players toward bulk repairs, narrowing it rewards daily attention.

Coolant

A rig consumes coolant continuously and stops the moment its fuel window lapses, whatever its durability. The fee is 10% of the yield actually produced, never a fixed token amount — a fixed fee becomes unpayable precisely when per-rig output falls, which is how these economies enter a death spiral.

The 24-hour fuel window is also what makes the contract tractable: it bounds how long a rig can go unsettled, so hashrate accounting never has to sweep every machine on chain.

Squirrel operations

Squirrels are a security force on a payroll, not landlords collecting rent. An unfed squirrel earns nothing.

Defence rank & algorithmic decay

Tier fixes a squirrel's alpha; D-Rank is a separate, mutable rating that decays one step at the close of every season as rig cryptography advances. Holding rank requires burning qBTC on a signature patch.

Implementation constraint

Rank decay must be applied as a multiplier at claim time. The staking structure buckets squirrels by alpha, so literally lowering a rank would require migrating every staked squirrel between buckets — unbounded work that cannot fit in one transaction.

07

Raids & defence

The passive 20% tax stays, but it is the floor of a squirrel's income rather than the whole of it. Raiding is where squirrel players actually play.

A squirrel spends 15 energy to raid a named rig. Success is weighted by alpha against the target's Firewall rating, and a successful raid takes a share of that rig's unclaimed yield. Failure costs the energy and nothing else.

P(success) = alpha / (alpha + firewall + 4)
stolen = min(pendingYield × 0.25, raidCap)

Rig owners answer by burning qBTC on Firewall upgrades, which decay one point per season. That exchange is the point: it is a standing sink funded by both sides of the game, and it converts squirrel income from rent into something earned.

Raid resolution is random and therefore commit–reveal, on the same two-block pattern as minting and unstaking.

08

Retention systems

A testnet is only informative if people keep showing up. These systems exist to make the second week more interesting than the first.

The daily loop

STEP 1
Read the panel

Durability, coolant window, energy, and any raids taken against you overnight.

STEP 2
Service the fleet

Refuel, repair, feed. One bulk transaction for the whole roster.

STEP 3
Act

Raid a rig, reinforce a firewall, or bank the difference.

STEP 4
Clear quests

Three dailies, refreshed on the season clock.

Uptime streak

Every consecutive day ending with all rigs fuelled and above 75% durability adds 1% hashrate, capped at 25%. A missed day resets it to zero.

StreakBonusDesign intent
7 days+7%Noticeable within the first week
25 days+25%Ceiling reached inside one season
Missed day0%A setback, not a reason to quit

The cap matters as much as the bonus. An uncapped streak compounds into exactly the permanent early-player advantage that seasonal decay exists to prevent.

Quests

Quests are the only track that pays a brand-new player immediately, which is what carries someone through their first empty-handed week.

CadenceExamplesShare of pool
DailyRefuel every rig · complete one raid · finish the day above 75%45%
WeeklySurvive seven days with no shutdown · steal from three distinct rigs30%
AchievementFirst Tier 3 squirrel · fuse a Gen 2 rig · burn 10,000 qBTC25%

Fusion

Two rigs of the same generation can be burned into one of the next, carrying a higher base hashrate and a slower decay rate. This is the endgame sink: it gives large holders something to spend on, removes NFTs from supply, and gives low-tier tokens a floor by making them fusion feedstock.

09

Seasons & ladders

Seasons run 30 days. Each closes with a rank decay, a leaderboard payout, and a fresh set of quests — a scheduled reason to return.

Season 1 pays out 210,000 qBTC across three ladders. Placement is by rank band, not by stake, so the top player takes a fixed 10% of a ladder rather than a multiple of what they deposited.

10

Claim vesting

Claimed qBTC vests linearly over seven days. Taking it early forfeits the unvested share, which is burned.

Withdrawn afterReceivedBurned
Immediately0%100%
1 day14%86%
3 days43%57%
5 days71%29%
7 days100%0%

This blunts dump pressure without locking anyone out, and the forfeited share is a sink that scales with impatience — it takes the most from exactly the behaviour that damages the token.

11

Anti-Ponzi ledger

An honest scorecard. Some of these are solved by the design; one is not, and pretending otherwise would defeat the purpose of writing it down.

PropertyStatusMechanism
Idle capital earns nothingSolvedCoolant windows and durability gate all mining yield
Rewards are not purely stake-weightedSolved40% of supply gated behind rank and activity
Early players cannot lock in dominanceSolvedSeasonal rank decay, capped streaks, firewall decay
Emission is not front-loadedSolved4%/season decay; 32% issued in year one, not 75%
Instant exit is discouragedSolvedSeven-day vesting with a burned forfeit
Standing demand beyond speculationPartialUpkeep, patches and the firewall arms race are perpetual sinks — but all internal
Token has a floor independent of new buyersUnsolvedRequires external revenue; see below
The honest limit

Every sink in this document is denominated in qBTC. Burning qBTC destroys a claim; it does not bring a single QRL into the system. The mechanisms above make the economy slower, fairer and harder to farm passively — they do not give the token a floor.

A floor requires value entering from outside. The two candidates are secondary-market royalties paid in QRL, and charging the coolant fee in QRL rather than qBTC — thematically exact, since a power bill is paid in real money. Route either into a staked treasury and grant qBTC a pro-rata redemption right, and the forced burns above start raising a floor instead of merely shrinking supply. That is a mainnet decision, not a testnet one.

12

What testnet proves

The point of running this on QRL Zond testnet v2 is to answer questions that no spreadsheet can.

  • Does the daily loop survive contact with real players? Simulated strategies always maintain their rigs. Real ones forget, go on holiday, and rage-quit after an explosion.
  • Is the maintenance burden fun or a chore? If bulk servicing a roster is tedious at ten rigs, it is unplayable at fifty — and the answer changes the whole upkeep cadence.
  • Do the ladders attract different players? If the same wallets top all three, the rank-gated 40% is stake-weighted in disguise.
  • Is the explosion risk thrilling or just punishing? A 10% daily loss chance reads very differently when it is your NFT.
  • How much gas does a day of play actually cost? Every mechanic here adds transactions, and commit–reveal doubles the risky ones.

Liquidity is deliberately out of scope for testnet. The AMM pool, its QRL pairing, and the redemption right all depend on answers this phase is meant to produce.
